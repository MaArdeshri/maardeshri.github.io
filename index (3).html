Introduction

My name is Masoud Ardeshiri, and I was born in Shiraz, Iran, in 1984. I am presently a doctoral researcher in Electrical Engineering, specializing in electric drives and power electronics. I received my B.Sc. degree in Electrical Engineering, with a specialization in Power Systems, from Iran Azad University, Shiraz Branch, in 2013. I subsequently received my M.Sc. degree in Electrical Engineering, also specializing in Power Systems, from Iran Azad University, Kazeroon Branch, in 2017. I am currently pursuing my Ph.D. at the Faculty of Electrical Engineering of Warsaw University of Technology, within the discipline of Automation, Electronics, Electrical Engineering and Space Technologies, with graduation expected in 2027. My research focuses on advanced control methods for synchronous and asynchronous electric drives, power electronic converters, battery energy-storage systems, and renewable-energy applications. I have experience developing energy-management strategies for wind-energy conversion systems, particularly for controlling and optimizing battery charging and discharging. I am also experienced in the modelling, simulation, analysis, control, and parameter optimization of electrical and renewable-energy systems, including wind and photovoltaic generation systems. My technical software expertise includes MATLAB/Simulink, PLECS, and PSIM. As part of my doctoral research project, I have developed and analyzed a comprehensive simulation model of a stand-alone doubly fed induction generator (DFIG) wind-energy conversion system integrated with a hybrid battery–supercapacitor energy-storage system. This project encompasses the modelling of the wind turbine, DFIG, back-to-back power-electronic converters, DC-link, three-phase load, and energy-storage interface; the design of coordinated control systems for stator voltage, load voltage, converter currents, and DC-link voltage; the development of energy-management strategies for battery charging and discharging; and the evaluation of system performance under balanced and unbalanced loading conditions across sub-synchronous and super-synchronous operating modes.
